Lesson 1Theory + tool

How your score works

Every score runs from 10 to 160 in steps of 5. Your overall score is the average of four subscores: Reading, Listening, Writing, Speaking.

Rules of the scoreboardOfficial
  • Overall = (Reading + Listening + Writing + Speaking) ÷ 4. If the average falls between two scores, it is rounded up to the next score ending in 0 or 5.
  • No question type has a fixed percentage. The test adapts, so your score is not just "number of right answers".
  • You also get four combined scores: Literacy (reading + writing), Comprehension (reading + listening), Conversation (listening + speaking), Production (writing + speaking).
  • Each skill is one quarter of the total. So the fastest way up is to lift your weakest skills. For you that is speaking and writing.

Lesson 2Read and Select5 sec · 15–18 words

Real word or fake?

You see one word. Is it a real English word? You have 5 seconds. No click means wrong.

How to winReading
  • Fake words look real. They follow English spelling rules.
  • Say the word silently. Do you recognise the sound?
  • Check the parts: a real stem + a real suffix (-ness, -ful, -tion) can still be fake if the combination is wrong: happiful.
  • Never sure? Trust your first feeling and move on.
Train itReading a lot in English is the only real preparation. Every word you met in a text becomes a "yes".

Be a grammar detectiveReading

The missing word can be a noun, verb, adjective or adverb. The sentence around it tells you which.

Lesson 2Read and Complete3 min · 3–6 texts

Repair the paragraph

Several words in a paragraph are cut in half. Type the missing endings. Every word is scored on its own.

Rules you can useReading
  • The title, the first sentence and the last sentence are complete. Read them first: they give you the topic.
  • Broken words are never next to each other.
  • Names (London, Sara) are never broken.
  • Do the easy words first, the hard ones last.
  • Check the grammar: past story → past tense (-ed); plural → -s.

Lesson 2Interactive Reading7–8 min · 6 questions

One story, six questions

Each passage comes with six questions in a fixed order. What you learn in one question helps with the next.

The six stepsReading
1Complete the Sentences · pick words for 3–10 gaps
2Complete the Passage · choose the missing sentence
3–4Highlight the Answer · drag to select the answer in the text
5Identify the Idea · main idea of the whole text
6Title the Passage · best title
  • The missing sentence must connect the two halves: check the sentence before and after the gap.
  • Highlight only the part that answers the question. You don't need a full sentence. Too much text loses points.
  • For main idea and title: reject options about one small detail.
  • Watch the clock. You need time for all six.

Lesson 4Listen and Type1 min · up to 3 plays

Type exactly what you hear

Your tutor reads each sentence aloud (that is the "audio"). You may hear it up to three times. Spelling and punctuation count.

Three-play strategyListening
Play 1Just listen. Catch the meaning.
Play 2Type everything you heard.
Play 3Check small words + endings.
  • Small words disappear in fast speech: to, of, the, has been, I'd, we'll.
  • Check endings: -s, -ed, and -n't.
  • Capital letter at the start, full stop or question mark at the end.
  • Grammar helps you hear: She hasn't decided… needs -ed.

A conversation on campus

You hear a situation, fill in missing parts, take part in a conversation by choosing replies, and finally write a short summary.

Why a reply is wrongOfficial tips

In Listen and Respond, wrong replies usually have one of these problems:

Off topicdoes not fit the situation
No matchdoes not answer what was said

Lesson 3Write About the Photo1 min · 3 photos

One minute, one strong paragraph

You type a description of a photo. From our trial: you already got 3 of 4 checks. Now we lock in all four, every time.

The 4-check formulaWriting
1 · Who + action + placeA woman is reading in a library.
2 · Detailadjectives + position: next to, behind
3 · Linkand, while, which, because
4 · Clean finishcapital letter + full stop
  • Aim for two or three full sentences in 60 seconds (about 30–45 words).
  • No long introductions like "This picture shows a situation that might be happening…". Start with the action.
  • Don't repeat the same word: in the trial, studying… studying became studying… preparing for the exam.

Lesson 3Interactive Writing30 s plan · 5 min + 3 min

Answer, then go deeper

Part 1: answer a question (5 min). Part 2: a follow-up on the same topic asks for a new idea (3 min). Both parts are scored together. The Writing Sample at the end of the test works like Part 1.

Build it before you write itWriting
AAnswer the question in one clear sentence.
RReason 1 + a specific example.
RReason 2 + example, or the other side ("Although…").
CConclusion: one sentence, new words.
  • Part 1 target: 100–150 words. Part 2 target: 60–90 words.
  • Part 2 asks about an angle you did not write about. Don't copy Part 1.
  • Use the 30 seconds to plan two reasons. Keep 30 seconds at the end to check spelling and full stops.

A question on the screen

PREP · four movesSpeaking
PPoint: answer directly. "I'd like to learn…"

Lesson 5Fluency skill

Lost word? Talk around it

On the test you will forget a word. Paraphrasing keeps you fluent. Describe the word on the card without saying it or the banned words. Your tutor guesses.

Paraphrase kitSpeaking + writing
  • Category: It's a kind of… / a type of…
  • Function: It's something you use to…
  • Person: It's the person who…
  • Place: It's the place where…
  • Compare: It's similar to…, but…
  • Look: It's usually made of… / It looks like…
